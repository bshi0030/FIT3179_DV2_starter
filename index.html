01

A changing wallet

Before looking at how Australians shop, we first need to understand how the household wallet itself has changed. The charts below move from the overall spending trend to the categories competing for a larger share of that wallet.

Australian household spending in 2025 dollars, 2012–2026

Monthly seasonally adjusted spending, adjusted using Australia's quarterly All Groups CPI and expressed in 2025 dollars. Use the dropdown to compare with nominal spending. For July 2026, June-quarter CPI is carried forward.

The big disruption: real household spending fell during the 2020 pandemic disruption. The default line removes general consumer-price inflation, so later growth should not be mistaken for price changes alone.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Monthly Household Spending Indicator. Data currently run to July 2026. Inflation adjustment: ABS All Groups CPI, Australia (Table 17); 2025 annual-average CPI base.

What Australians spend on has not moved evenly

Cells show percentage-point changes in each category’s share of spending relative to 2013—not the size of each category.

Read the movement: warm and cool cells indicate gains and losses in a category’s share since 2013. Composition changes are separate from rising prices.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Monthly Household Spending Indicator, COICOP categories.

How the household budget splits between needs and wants

02

Shopping moves online

Australians are spending more online, but the digital shift looks different depending on where people live. State growth, total market size and local shopping hotspots reveal three different geographic stories.

Where is online shopping growing fastest?

Colour shows year-on-year growth in online spending across each state and territory in 2025.

Western Australia leads: online spending grew 18% year-on-year, compared with 10% in the ACT.

Source: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, p. 15.

Where is online spending highest per resident?

Circle size now represents 2025 online expenditure divided by the state’s June 2025 resident population. Tooltips retain the total spending figure.

A fairer geographic comparison: spending per resident controls for state population; it is not spending per online shopper.

Sources: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, p. 15; ABS, National, State and Territory Population, June 2025.

Online-shopping hotspots extend beyond the biggest capitals

Australia Post highlights different locations depending on total parcel volume, shopping activity per person and recent growth.

Regional places matter too: Toowoomba and Mackay rank among the top locations by volume, while Pinjarra and Fraser Rise stand out for growth.

Source: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, p. 16. Point locations are approximate locality centres used only to position the named postcodes on the map.

The geography is only part of the story. Next, we move from where Australians shop to what they buy and how those choices differ by generation.

03

Your lifestyle shapes your cart

Geography is only part of the story. Age, shopping frequency and digital habits also shape how Australians buy. Different generations participate in eCommerce in distinctly different ways, while the categories filling the online cart continue to evolve.

What fills Australia's online cart?

Rectangle size represents 2025 online spending, while colour intensity shows year-on-year growth. Large categories are not always the fastest growing.

Biggest does not mean fastest: online marketplaces attracted the most spending at $18.9b, while books, stationery and multimedia recorded the strongest growth at 24.1%.

Source: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, p. 12.

Five generations, five shopping profiles

Each cell prints the actual value. Colour compares generations within the same measure, not across columns with different units.

Digital adoption and spending are not the same thing: Millennials shop online most frequently and spend the most online, while Gen X records the largest average basket.

Source: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, pp. 7–8 and 13–14.

Online is growing, but stores still dominate

The share of spending that occurs online differs by generation, but in-store spending still represents the majority for every group.

Millennials lead the online shift: 28.5% of their spending occurs online, compared with 14.3% among Builders. Even so, more than 70% of spending remains in-store for every generation.

Source: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, p. 13.

Beyond digital convenience, value and reuse are changing purchasing decisions.

04

Value has a new meaning

Shopping does not always mean buying new. Second-hand purchasing is an established part of the Australian consumer landscape.

Nearly half of Australians buy second-hand

Each square represents one percentage point. This illustrative grid shows the 46% reported by Australia Post; it does not show individual survey respondents.

46 in every 100: the reported share of Australians buying second-hand items at least once a year.

Source: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, p. 29, based on its 2025 consumer survey.

Which second-hand products appeal most?

Books and music lead the reported categories. People could express interest in more than one category.

Source: Australia Post eCommerce Report 2026, p. 29.

From the household budget to the digital basket and second-hand purchases, Australia's shopping story is shaped by prices, place and personal priorities.
